Accountability in the Apps We Use

The digital world wasn’t built with kids in mind. We’re here to change that — starting with transparency, education, and a clear vision for what comes next.

Kids get phones early. They join apps early. They encounter risks early. But the protections they need often come late — or not at all.

ReadyOnline is committed to helping families understand the gaps, navigate them today, and push the industry toward a safer future.

Where We Are Today

Right now, every major app — Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, Discord, Spotify, Roblox, YouTube and more — allows a child to create an adult account, even when:

  • The device is set up as a child account
  • The operating system knows the child’s real age
  • The parent approved the download through Apple Family Sharing or Android Family Link

Apps rely on self‑reported age, which kids can bypass in seconds. Parents assume protections are in place. Kids assume they’re safe. Apps assume nothing. This is the accountability gap.

What Parents Need to Know

Behind the Scenes

  • Apps do not automatically switch to teen mode, even on child devices.
  • Apps do not read the child’s real age from Apple or Android.
  • Risky features are often enabled by default: DMs, location sharing, chat, discovery.
  • Parents are rarely notified when these features turn on.

Why It Matters

Our teen survey shows kids access major platforms between ages 8–13, long before most parents expect. That’s why ReadyOnline focuses on helping families configure, clean up, and stay aware of the digital spaces their kids use.

Teens Want Better Tools Too

This isn’t just a parent concern — older teens want better safety tools for themselves.

Our survey shows that teens over 16 want to learn how to protect their accounts, spot scams, understand privacy, recognize manipulation, and support friends who may be struggling online. They’re not asking for more monitoring. They’re asking for clearer design, honest settings, and protections that don’t require digging through hidden menus.

Teens want autonomy — and they want apps to make safe choices the default. ReadyOnline is built for them too: teaching the skills, reflexes, and awareness they want to develop, early and often.

What ReadyOnline Can Do Right Now

Practical Help for Families

  • Clear setup guides for each app and platform.
  • How to fix accounts that were set up as adult accounts.
  • Education on Apple Family and Android Family tools.
  • Ongoing updates on new risks and scams.
  • Conversation prompts for parents and teens.

Building Reflexes, Not Just Rules

We don’t just teach “settings.” We teach digital reflexes — pausing, checking surroundings, and making safer choices before tapping forward. Technology can help, but it can’t replace awareness.

The Future We Expect: Child Mode API

We believe in a simple, universal standard: if the device is flagged as a child device, apps must automatically enable child protections.

Vision · Child Mode API

A basic signal from the operating system — “this is a child account” — should be enough for apps to turn on teen/child protections by default. No self‑reported age. No hidden menus. No loopholes.

This is technically possible. It’s overdue. ReadyOnline will champion this vision as we grow.

What We Expect from Apps

We’re not attacking companies. We’re setting expectations — clearly, constructively, and publicly.

Meta · Instagram & Messenger
  • Automatic teen mode on child devices.
  • Private accounts by default for minors.
  • DM restrictions for under‑18 users.
  • No self‑reported age overrides.
Discord
  • Teen mode with safer defaults.
  • No NSFW servers for minors.
  • No DMs from strangers by default.
  • Safer onboarding for young users.
Snapchat
  • Snap Map off by default for minors.
  • Quick Add disabled for child accounts.
  • Private accounts mandatory for under‑18.
TikTok
  • Teen mode auto‑on for child devices.
  • Content filters mandatory for minors.
  • No birthday bypass to access adult features.
Spotify
  • No chat/social features for minors by default.
  • Clear disclosures to parents about social features.
  • Teen mode for shared family accounts.
YouTube & Gaming Platforms
  • Autoplay off for minors.
  • Restricted/teen modes on by default.
  • Safer chat and voice defaults in games.

How ReadyOnline Will Grow Into Advocacy

We’re not launching petitions or lobbying campaigns today. We’re building the foundation.

  • Sharing aggregated insights with platforms about what teens and parents need.
  • Offering safety curriculum and teen‑tested modules that apps can reference.
  • Providing app configuration guidance for families and schools.
  • Supporting OS‑level standards that apps must honor.
  • Building a coalition of parents, educators, and teens over time.

This is a long‑term vision, not a day‑one initiative. But it starts here — with clarity and expectations.
