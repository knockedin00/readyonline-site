Platform accountability

Families deserve clearer protection.

ReadyOnline helps families develop instinct. Platforms still have a responsibility to make safety visible, consistent, and meaningful.

See the standards → Parent module

Baseline expectation

Safety should not depend on guessing.

Clear child and teen protections
Meaningful age enforcement
Transparent data and safety settings
What families should expect

Standards that are clear enough to use.

Families should not need an expert-level understanding of each app to find the safety settings that matter.

Age protections

Age should not be a self-reported detail that silently removes safeguards.

Clear controls

Protections, data choices, and reporting paths should be understandable and visible.

Consistent baseline

Safety should not change wildly from one platform to another or depend on hidden defaults.

App-by-app gaps

What parents may think versus what can happen.

These are conversation starters and awareness points, not a substitute for each platform’s current settings and policies.

PlatformWhat families may assumeGap to discuss
InstagramOnly real friends can find a teen.Fake or recently created accounts and “sponsored” offers can use DMs to create pressure.
SnapchatMessages disappearing means they are safe.Streaks, private stories, and Snap Map can create urgency, secrecy, and location exposure.
TikTokIt is only short videos.Stranger collab requests and fake “TikTok staff” outreach can seek account information.
DiscordIt is only for gaming friends.Nitro links, private channels, screen-share requests, and downloads can move risk quickly.
SpotifyIt is only music.Its social features and group context can sit below adult awareness and still shape pressure.
RobloxIt is a game with child-friendly spaces.Trades, Robux, and private-server invitations can be used to create obligation or isolation.
YouTubeIt is only videos.Comments, creator impersonation, links, and misinformation can create risks beyond viewing.
