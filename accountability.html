Accountability in the Apps We Use

Kids and teens use the same apps adults do — but the protections aren’t the same. Most major platforms allow children to create adult accounts even when devices are set up as child accounts or parents approve downloads through family settings. This page outlines the gaps, expectations, and the standards we believe every platform should meet.

Where Platforms Fall Short

Adult Accounts for Kids

Apps like Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, Discord, Spotify, Roblox, and YouTube allow children to create adult accounts with no meaningful verification.

No Real Age Enforcement

Platforms rely on self‑reported birthdays, making it easy for kids to bypass protections.

Opaque Safety Settings

Parents cannot see what protections are active, what data is collected, or what risks their child is exposed to.

Inconsistent Policies

Safety features vary wildly between apps, leaving families confused and unprotected.

What Families Should Expect

Clear Child Mode

Every app should offer a true child mode with locked‑in protections and transparent controls.

Verified Age Controls

Platforms must verify age in a way that doesn’t rely on self‑reported birthdays.

Transparent Data Use

Families deserve to know what data is collected, how it’s used, and how to limit it.

Consistent Safety Standards

Every major app should meet baseline protections for minors — not optional or hidden ones.

Our Commitment

ReadyOnline advocates for stronger protections, clearer standards, and honest communication from the platforms kids use every day. We believe accountability starts with transparency — and families deserve both.
